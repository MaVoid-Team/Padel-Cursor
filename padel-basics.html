Padel Basics

Learn everything you need to know about padel, from the basic rules to advanced strategies.

What is padel all about?

Padel was invented in Mexico in 1969 and has since grown to become one of the most popular sports in Spain. Today, it's one of the fastest-growing sports in the world, with Miami serving as the epicenter of padel in the United States.

Padel is a mix of racquet sports played on turf with a net and four glass walls. The ball stays in play off the walls, making it a dynamic and exciting game. It's a social sport played in doubles, making it easy to learn, fun, and challenging for players of all skill levels.

What are the rules?

Scoring

Scoring is like tennis: Love, 15, 30, 40, Deuce. There's a "golden point" for ties. Sets go to 6 games (win by 2), with a tiebreaker at 6 all.

The Serve

Serve underhand, bounce behind the service line, hit crosscourt into the opposite service box. Strike at or below waist level. You get two serves (including lets).

In and Out

Ball must hit the turf first to be in. Hitting the glass or fence first on the opposite side is out. You can return balls off the glass/fence on your own side, but a serve hitting the fence after the turf is out.

Playing off the Walls

You can let the ball bounce off the glass before hitting it. You can even use the walls on your side for shots. This is what makes padel unique and exciting!

Winning Points

Points are won when a ball cannot be returned over the net or if it bounces twice. The ball must always go over the net to the opponent's side.

Pro Tips for Beginners

Start with the Basics

Focus on getting the ball over the net consistently before worrying about power or spin. Control is more important than speed.

Use the Walls

Don't be afraid to let the ball bounce off the walls. This is what makes padel unique and can be a strategic advantage.

Communicate with Your Partner

Padel is a doubles game, so communication is key. Call out shots and work together as a team.

Practice Your Serve

A good serve sets up the point. Practice your underhand serve to get it consistently in the service box.
